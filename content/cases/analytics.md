## Situation

Two enterprise products, hosted separately with separate logins and basic Google Analytics on a few pages. They had been trying to make a cross-sell bundle work for over a year, and nobody knew how customers used either one. A couple of PMs and I mapped what we needed to measure and asked engineering how long it would take in the approved tool. Two years.

## My role

I treated it as a tool problem. A four-week vendor evaluation landed on Fullstory, which needs one commit and is then configured in the UI. One developer, half a day per product.

I had no headcount for the configuration, so I asked another team’s manager for a few hours a week of Andrew, an underused launcher in the intern-to-FTE program, and taught him to build dashboards against our measurement map. I built the views for engineering, support, success and sales, with one or two champions from each team shaping them and then teaching their own people.

## What changed

Usable dashboards at six weeks, full coverage at three months, nine functions using them. Engineering and support reproduced defects together from session replays. Success and sales checked usage health before every customer call. Andrew got the promotion and the full-time offer.

Then the data got used. After an acquisition, two product leaders spent months disagreeing about where to integrate, and the IC teams were stuck waiting. I combined our usage data with the acquired product’s reporting and scored features on retention lift, churn effort and anchor strength. The two workflows both leaders assumed were the core turned out to be reactive and concentrated near churn; two others were the habitual ones. Both leaders accepted it, and the IC teams left with a full plan of work they could start.

## What’s still hard

The faster defect diagnosis was reported to me, not measured. The bigger integration questions were still open when the post-acquisition reorg came.
